## EMAIL 2 — Day 1
**Subject:** The thing you've been calling stress
**Preview:** It has another name. And naming it changes everything.

---

Hey {{contact.first_name}},

Yesterday I mentioned Pillar 1: Powerlessness.

Most men resist that word. They're not powerless — they're successful. They built something. They show up. They provide.

But here's the thing: powerlessness isn't about weakness. It's about the specific feeling that surfaces when something is out of control — and what your brain does about it automatically, below your awareness.

I called it stress for fifteen years.

I played video games for hours. Not because I enjoyed them — because they were the one place I could win. I overate. I worked obsessively. I withdrew from my wife when I didn't mean to. And I called all of it just being tired.

The honest name for it is the False Power Move Cycle. And until a man can name his version of it, he can't interrupt it.

Here's the question from the guide that does the most work:

What do you reach for when you feel out of control?

Sit with that for five minutes today. Not while driving, not while checking email. Actually sit with it.

Tomorrow: the discipline most high-achieving men are only doing half of.

Pillar 2 is about the gap between who you are when people are watching and who you are when they're not.

Most high-achieving men are outstanding at public accountability. They deliver. They show up. They keep their word to everyone around them.

And privately, they are falling apart.

Not dramatically. Not in a way anyone can see. Just the slow erosion of a man who hasn't built his private foundation — who asks his wife and kids and team to receive a man he hasn't yet become himself.

The shift happens when the private and public man start to merge. When who you are in your first hour of the day — before anyone is watching — starts to match the man you want everyone to see.

## EMAIL 4 — Day 5
**Subject:** The door you keep forcing
**Preview:** God may be keeping it closed on purpose.

---

Hey {{contact.first_name}},

Pillar 3 cuts against something most driven men have built their entire identity around.

We decide. We plan. We execute. We succeed.

And then we ask God to bless what we've already done.

That's not co-creation. That's building alone and asking for an endorsement after the fact.

The sequence that actually works inverts that. Foundation first. Honest prayer before the plan, not after. Being still enough to notice which doors are actually opening — because they open quietly, not loudly.

I felt a nudge to look up pool builder websites in my area. I filled out a form. Within a year we went from $500,000 to $1.5 million in revenue.

I didn't force that door. I was still enough to notice it.

## EMAIL 5 — Day 7
**Subject:** What your kids will say at your funeral
**Preview:** Not about what you built. About who you were.

---

Hey {{contact.first_name}},

Pillar 4 asks the question most successful men spend their entire lives avoiding.

Not because they don't care about the answer — but because they're afraid of it.

If income were removed as a measure of success — what else would tell you this year mattered?

Most men go quiet when I ask them this. The silence is useful information. It means they've been measuring themselves against a single metric for so long that removing it leaves nothing.

Purpose doesn't need to be invented. In almost every man it is already there — covered by urgency, obligation, and the accumulated weight of a life built trying to prove he was enough.

The question that uncovers it faster than anything else I've found:

What do you want your kids to say at your funeral — not about what you built, but about who you were?

Write the answer that makes you feel something. Not the one that sounds good. That answer is a compass.
