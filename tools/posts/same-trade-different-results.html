I've spent my whole career in one trade: building swimming pools. And I've watched something play out over and over again.

Two companies do the same work. Same industry, same kinds of customers, same materials, often in the same market. One builds an amazing, robust business that keeps growing. The other never seems to get past a certain point, year after year.

Why?

I've thought about this a lot. I don't think the answer is talent, luck, or market conditions. I believe it comes down to two things, and they both live inside the owner.

Key #1: The size of the vision

The greatest reason one business excels over another is simply the vision of the owner and the leaders, and how dedicated they are to that grand vision.

It's almost a law:

The lesser the vision, the lesser the results. The grander the vision, the grander the results.

A business rarely outgrows the picture in its leader's head. If the owner sees a small shop that pays the bills, that's usually what gets built. If he sees something bigger, something that serves more families, develops more people, and makes a real mark, the business starts bending toward that picture.

That's the absolute first key to success.

Key #2: Complete dedication to making it real

Not far behind the vision is complete dedication to actualizing it.

A vision without commitment is just a nice idea. What turns it into a business is the leader buying in fully to the steps it takes to get there, especially the hard, boring, uncomfortable ones.

And here's what every owner needs to understand: your team buys in exactly as much as you do.

The more the leader commits to the steps, the more everyone else in the organization will. If the leader falters in the execution, everyone else will too. Your people aren't listening to your speeches. They're watching what you actually do when it gets hard.

Why I'm bringing this up

Because I've seen it in my own businesses.

My companies have risen and fallen with my ability to get the vision, and then, once I had it, with my ability to buy in fully. My path has been full of ups and downs. And the bigger my businesses have gotten, the heavier those ups and downs have felt.

When things don't go the way I think they should, I have to choose how to react. I have to ask whether my actions are in line with the vision or not.

I'll be honest: plenty of times I've gotten weary in the pursuit. I've dropped off in focus and commitment. But the vision has never left me. And over and over, I've found the strength to keep going, a lot of the time in blind faith, not knowing whether anything we were trying would actually work.

What growth does to a leader

As a leader moves through growth, crazy things happen to him.

The kind of growth you experience can be so foreign that your old beliefs about what's possible come rushing back in and blow up the success. Doubt shows up. Old patterns show up. You start focusing on the risks instead of the gains, because you're stepping into territory you've never seen before.

I know this because it happened to me. Moving into new levels of life and business was hard, because I kept letting my old ways and my doubts come in and take over. I was afraid of what I didn't know.

That's exactly why I created BILD and the methods inside it. Not because I figured it all out, but because I needed a way to keep growing on the inside fast enough to handle what was happening on the outside.

The toughest path there is

I honestly believe the life of a business owner is the toughest path a person can take.

The pursuit of growth, and everything that comes with it, can be the most amazing experience of your life. It can also be the most terrifying. Often in the same week.

What will you say when the work is done?

When it's all said and done and you finish your work, what will you want to say about it?

Will it be: I did the best I could no matter what. I ran into the unknown with total faith, and I experienced hell and heaven on the journey.

Or will it be: I settled in where I felt safe from any more disturbance or threats, and I came up way short of what could have been.

Here's my challenge to you:

  1. Get your vision. Make it grand enough to pull you forward.
  2. Commit to never wavering in the pursuit of it, especially on the days you feel weary.
  3. When you've reached it, do it again.

Life isn't about stopping, or thinking that one day you'll be done. You won't be done until the day your heart stops beating.

So go all in on your path. Be great. Not to compete, not to win a prize, not to prove you're better than anyone else. Go all in so you can say you never gave in to the story that you couldn't do it, and so you can say you lived the fullest life you could.

The rewards that come from that are ones no one else will ever fully understand.

What's the vision you've been holding back on?
