I wrote the 6 Pillars. I teach them. I build my coaching around them.

And this week, when I sat down and read them again, I saw places in my own life where I'm not fully living them.

That's not a confession of failure. It's the whole point. I'm always working on these pillars, but working on something and being fully aligned with it are two different things. Going back and reading them slowly, with honest eyes, showed me exactly where my life could use some recalibrating.

What's inside creates what's outside

This is so important for us as men: we have to keep checking ourselves.

The experiences we have out in the world, in our marriages, with our kids, in our businesses, and with God, are created by what's happening inside us. When something feels off out there, the first place to look is in here.

The problem is that it's easy to slide back into old patterns. Patterns I've already decided I don't want running my life anymore. They don't announce themselves. They just quietly take the wheel again when I'm tired, busy, or stressed.

That's why reviewing the pillars matters. I haven't fully adopted every one of them into my life yet. But there's something powerful about going back, reading them again, and doing an honest dive into my relationship with each one. It shows me the work that's actually in front of me, not the work I assume I've already finished.

An honest check-in, pillar by pillar

Here's how I'm walking through them. One pillar, one honest question. I don't answer in the way I'd answer in public. I answer the way I'd answer at 2am when the house is quiet.

1. Powerlessness. When I feel out of control, what do I reach for? Withdrawal, overwork, harshness, distraction? Naming my powerlessness isn't weakness. It's the honest foundation everything else gets built on.

2. Accountability. Does the man I am in private match the man people see in public? The gap between those two is what my wife feels and what my kids sense, even when no one says it out loud.

3. Create With Him. Am I building with God, or deciding first and asking Him to bless it afterward? Where am I trying to force open a door He might be keeping closed for a reason?

4. Purpose. Am I chasing achievement, or living for what I'm actually here for? They can look the same from the outside. They feel very different on the inside.

5. Forgiveness. Is there someone I think of with heat or heaviness I can't quite explain? Is there something I've apologized for but never fully forgiven myself for? Unforgiveness is a ceiling. The other pillars can only reach as high as it allows.

6. Imagination. Am I still dreaming, or have I slipped into just managing my marriage, my business, and my faith? Anxiety shrinks a man's vision. Faith pulls it back open.

When I read these honestly, a few of them sting. That sting is useful. It's a map to where my attention needs to go next.

Small adjustments, big openings

Recalibrating doesn't mean tearing your life down and starting over. It means noticing where you've drifted and making small, honest corrections.

The little ways we work on ourselves, and practice what we see we need to work on, open up things we couldn't have planned:

  • New opportunities to learn. Every gap I find is a place to grow.
  • Deeper connection with others. The more honest I am with myself, the more honest and present I can be with my wife, my kids, and the men around me.
  • Closer alignment with purpose. Each correction brings me back toward what I was made for.

These principles aren't new. So many men and women before us have shown that this is the sure way to more peace, more purpose, more growth, and the kind of sincere, honest experiences that make life feel full and exciting.

And when you're aligned, something shifts. Today stops feeling like everything is working against you. It starts feeling like everything is working in your favor.

Your turn

Here's my challenge to you, man to man. This week, read through the pillars, or just the questions above. Don't rush it. Pick the one that stings the most, and give it your attention for the next seven days.

Not all six. Just one. Little by little is how a man actually changes.

Which one stung for you?
